ganglia–thalamus network model of the 6-OHDA lesioned rat, used it to establish
the frequency dependence of STN DBS, deconstructed the STN-DBS-evoked cortical

## The standard this seat applies

Four demands recur across our three papers. Each is stated here with what backs
it, including where the answer is "nothing but our own habit".

**1. Every parameter carries a source, and the tuned ones are named as tuned.**
Kumaravelu 2016 Table 1 gives all eleven transmission delays with a citation each
(Kita & Kita 2011 for the three corticofugal ones; Nakanishi et al. 1987 for
dStr→GPi and STN→GPi; Kita & Kitai 1991 for idStr→GPe and STN→GPe; Fujimoto &
Kita 1993 for GPe→STN; Nakanishi et al. 1991 for GPe→GPi; Xu et al. 2008 for
GPi→TH; Walker et al. 2012 for TH→CTX), and Tables 2–11 give every conductance
and gating parameter. *Evidence basis:* the delay sources are rat in-vitro slice
and in-vivo preparations, each checkable. But §3 of the same paper states plainly
that "the values of several parameters were tuned to match the responses evoked
in the basal ganglia by cortical stimulation in rats (Kita and Kita 2011)", and
that the GPe and GPi applied currents were *fixed* at 3 µA/cm² to produce rates
similar to those measured in rats. The standard is therefore not "every value is
measured" but "every value is either sourced or declared tuned".

**2. The model is validated against electrophysiology before it is used to
answer the question.** Kumaravelu 2016 §3 compares model PSTHs in Str, STN, GPe
and GPi to cortical-stimulation PSTHs recorded in control and 6-OHDA rats (Kita
& Kita 2011, Figs 2–5) and model rates to the same study's measured rates
(Fig 6), *before* any DBS is applied; Kumaravelu 2018 states that "Model
parameters were not altered in any way to fit any of the in vivo STN
DBS-generated cEP responses". *Evidence basis:* the comparison targets are
published rat recordings; the discipline of not fitting the validation target is
a methodological commitment, not an experimental finding.

**3. DBS is represented as activation of a fixed set of neural elements, one
event per pulse.** Kumaravelu 2016 §2.6: "DBS was modeled by applying
intracellular current pulses in all STN model neurons so that every pulse evoked
one action potential ... (amplitude 300 µA/cm², duration 0.3 ms)". Kumaravelu
2018 Methods: monophasic pulses into 60% of L5 axon terminals, "such that each
pulse produced one spike", with the activated proportion varied in 20% steps as
the model's representation of stimulus amplitude. *Evidence basis:* the "one
spike per pulse" rule is our convention, justified in Kumaravelu 2016 §5.4 by
citations we did not read (Anderson et al. 2003; Hashimoto et al. 2003) for
activation of efferent axons. The *fixed set* half has direct evidence in our own
data: Kumaravelu 2018 Fig 5 shows the pulse-triggered cEP is stable pulse by
pulse across a 60-s, ~215-pulse epoch in all six rats, and Fig 11B shows that
changing the activated proportion changes which cEP components appear.

**4. A DBS mechanism is judged by its dose–response, not at a single operating
point.** Kumaravelu 2016 §4.3 and Fig 11: STN DBS below 40 Hz did not
substantially change GPi 7–35 Hz power; power fell gradually between 50 and
130 Hz and saturated above 150 Hz. Su 2019 Fig 5 reproduces the profile in the
same network. *Evidence basis:* the profile is a model result; the claim that it
parallels symptom suppression rests on three rat studies (Li et al. 2012;
McConnell et al. 2012; Ryu et al. 2013) that we cite from our own paper but did
not read. What we vouch for from our own data is that the profile is a cheap
falsification target our model passed without being fitted to it.

## Points

### 4.1 The subcortical delay set is ours, and the one cross-species check we can offer supports the transfer

**What BGM_22 does:** `parameters.csv` column `BGM_v07_p01` sets seven
subcortical pathway delays (`str_d1__snr` 4, `str_d*__gpe_*` 5, `stn__snr` 1.5,
`stn__gpe_*` 2, `gpe_proto__stn` 4, `gpe_proto__snr` 3, `snr__thal` 5 ms), read
into the model by `BGM._set_connections()`. We extracted the column with the
`csv` module and confirmed every value. `model_v07.md` §5 records that these
match our Table 1 and attaches two caveats: they are rat values in a human fit,
and Liénard et al. 2024's macaque estimates disagree by up to a factor of two on
the striatofugal pathways.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2016 Table 1, the same seven values plus CTX→Str
5.1 ms, CTX→STN 5.9 ms and TH→CTX 5.6 ms, each with a source.

**Evidence behind our choice:** rat preparations throughout — Nakanishi et al.
1987 (intracellular recording from rat SNr neurons in an in-vitro slice,
responses to subthalamic stimulation) for dStr→GPi 4 ms and STN→GPi 1.5 ms;
Kita & Kitai 1991 (rat globus pallidus, intracellular, responses to neostriatal,
subthalamic and nigral stimulation) for idStr→GPe 5 ms and STN→GPe 2 ms;
Fujimoto & Kita 1993 for GPe→STN 4 ms; Nakanishi et al. 1991 (rat
entopeduncular nucleus in vitro) for GPe→GPi 3 ms; Xu et al. 2008 for
GPi→TH 5 ms. These are measured latencies, not tuned values.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** BGM_22 could not have done
better from our literature, and the cross-species worry is smaller than the
project fears. The one direct rat-versus-human comparison in our reading is
Kumaravelu 2018 Fig 7 and its surrounding text: the same STN-DBS-evoked cortical
components have latencies R1 ~1.4 ms, R2 8.33 ± 0.73 ms, R3 27.77 ± 2.42 ms in
rat, against R1 ~1 ms, R2 5.7 ± 1.1 ms, R3 22.2 ± 1.8 ms in a human patient
(Walker et al. 2012, as reproduced in that figure) — human latencies are
*shorter* by roughly 20–30% on the polysynaptic components, because a ~12-fold
higher conduction velocity (~5 m/s in rat, ~60 m/s required in human) more than
compensates for the longer distance. Two honest limits: that comparison covers
the corticosubthalamic and cortico-thalamo-cortical routes, not the striatofugal
or pallidal delays BGM_22 actually borrows, and it is one human subject. Still,
it points the opposite way from "human brains are bigger, so scale the delays
up", and a tens-of-percent error on a millisecond quantity is invisible through a
2.31 s TR. We ask only that the provenance be recorded as "Kumaravelu et al.
2016 Table 1, rat, sources therein", which `model_v07.md` now nearly does.
**Difference, not deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** low — the delays are sub-TR and cannot move a BOLD
correlation, but the provenance matters for the write-up.

### 4.2 The DBS effect is exactly linear in stimulation frequency, by construction

**What BGM_22 does:** `DBSstimulator._set_constants` (`CompNeuroPy/dbs.py`)
defines `pulse(time_ms) = ite(modulo(time_ms*1000, 1000000./dbs_pulse_frequency_Hz)
< dbs_pulse_width_us, 1., 0.)`, and every DBS term in
`add_DBS_to_spiking_neuron_model` is gated by `pulse(t)`: the somatic shunt
`+ pulse(t)*dbs_on*dbs_depolarization*neg(-90 - v)`, the axon-spike condition
`pulse(t)*dbs_on*unif_var_dbs1 > 1-prob_axon_spike`, and through the latter the
antidromic `axon_reset`. The uniform draws are per-neuron, per-timestep, and
`DBS.md` records that axon spikes ignore refractoriness. There is no adaptation,
depression or pulse-to-pulse interaction anywhere in the mechanism. The
consequence is arithmetic: the expected DBS-injected perturbation over any
interval is the per-pulse perturbation times the number of pulses, i.e. exactly
proportional to `dbs_pulse_frequency_Hz`, with no threshold and no saturation
up to the 1/dt quantisation limit.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2016 §4.3 and Fig 11 sweep STN DBS from 5 to 200 Hz
in the same network and report a thresholded, saturating profile: no substantial
change in GPi 7–35 Hz power below 40 Hz, gradual decline between 50 and 130 Hz,
saturation above 150 Hz. Su 2019 Fig 5 reproduces it (beta power flat to ~40 Hz,
falling steeply to ~90 Hz, flat above ~110 Hz) and uses that curve as the plant
characteristic for a closed-loop controller.

**Evidence behind our choice:** the sigmoid is an emergent property of our model,
not an imposed one. Its experimental anchor, as cited in Kumaravelu 2016 §4.3, is
that the same frequency profile describes symptom suppression in 6-OHDA rats (Li
et al. 2012; McConnell et al. 2012; Ryu et al. 2013); §5.2 adds the
human/primate counterpart (>100 Hz effective, <50 Hz usually ineffective, citing
Fogelson et al. 2005 and Timmermann et al. 2004). We read none of those five
primary sources and report them as our paper's citation trail; what we read is
our own Fig 11E, a model result.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** BGM_22 fits at one frequency
(125 Hz, sub-01's clinical setting per
`experimental_data/berlin_data/vta/stim_settings.csv`), squarely in the
therapeutic regime, so nothing in the fit itself depends on this. But the
project's claim is mechanistic — "what DBS did inside the basal ganglia" — and a
mechanism linear in frequency with no threshold contradicts the best-established
quantitative fact about STN DBS. It also forecloses the only cheap external
check available: a frequency sweep costs one constant and would provably return
a straight line here. We must state the limit of our own proposal: BGM_22 has no
pathological oscillation to suppress (`input_streams/README.md` §4.3 and the
all-zero correlation settings in `parameters.py` leave no beta in the inputs)
and reads out BOLD at 2.31 s, so it could not measure our dose–response even if
the mechanism supported one. The defensible ask is therefore narrow: record in
`DBS.md` and in the write-up that the DBS representation is a phenomenological
per-pulse perturbation calibrated at a single frequency, linear in frequency by
construction, and not to be extrapolated to other stimulation settings. A
mechanistic frequency dependence would require a term with memory across pulses,
of which the current equation set has none. **Experimentally grounded
deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** high — it bounds what the fitted DBS parameters may be
called in the paper, which is exactly the project's deliverable.

### 4.3 The antidromically invaded GPe and SNr neurons are a fresh random subset at every pulse

**What BGM_22 does:** `_set_antidromic` in `CompNeuroPy/dbs.py` sets, on each
afferent population of the stimulated STN, `pop.antidromic = 1` and
`pop.antidromic_prob = np.mean(stimulated_population.dbs_on)` — 0.4 here — and
on the passing-fibre presynaptic population `antidromic_prob =
passing_fibres_strength`. The reset itself is
`v += ite(unif_var_dbs2 < antidromic_prob, dbs_on*antidromic*(-v + c), 0)`, and
`unif_var_dbs2` is a **local** `Uniform(0,1)` inserted into the equations block,
so it is redrawn per neuron per timestep. `_set_dbs_on` gives `dbs_on = 1` to
every non-excluded, non-stimulated population, so all 100 `gpe_proto:putamen`
neurons are eligible and a different ~40% of the ones that emitted an axon spike
are invaded on each pulse. The stimulated STN itself is the exception and is
handled correctly: its `dbs_on` is the fixed 0/1 `dbs_on_array` created once by
`_create_dbs_on_array`, so its activated set is anatomically fixed.

**What we do:** the activated elements are a fixed set. Kumaravelu 2016 §2.6
activates all ten STN neurons on every pulse; Kumaravelu 2018 Methods activates
a fixed 60% of L5 axon terminals, "such that each pulse produced one spike", and
varies that fixed proportion in 20% steps to represent amplitude.

**Evidence behind our choice:** which elements lie inside a given VTA is a
modelling assumption in both papers, but the *reliability* half is measured.
Kumaravelu 2018 Fig 5 plots the pulse-triggered cEP for all ~215 pulses of a
60-s 4.5-Hz epoch in six awake rats and reports "the remarkable stability of all
3 cEP response components (R1, R2, and R3) across all 6 rats"; Table 1 gives R1
latency 1.35 ± 0.07 ms. An evoked response that reproducible pulse after pulse
is produced by the same axons each time, not by a fresh random 40%. Against that
we report the narrowing our own source imposes: the same Discussion notes that
"antidromic spike propagation in axons of the hyperdirect pathway was faithful
only for low stimulation frequencies (Li et al. 2012)", and Fig 4B1 shows R1
amplitude significantly reduced at 130 Hz relative to 9 Hz (P = 0.011,
Friedman's ANOVA, Dunn-Bonferroni post hoc). So at 125 Hz per-pulse activation is
genuinely unreliable — but *stochastically thinned within a fixed axon
population*, not resampled across the whole nucleus.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** the difference is not a detail
at the level BGM_22 reads out. A fixed subset produces persistent heterogeneity
in GPe — a strongly perturbed 40% and an untouched 60%, which is what Kumaravelu
2016 §4.2 and Fig 10 describe as a greater *number* of pallidal neurons being
influenced by high-frequency stimulation, with both increases and decreases in
rate — whereas a per-pulse redraw applies a diluted,
identical-in-expectation perturbation to every GPe neuron and cannot produce that
heterogeneity at all. The fix is cheap and local: draw the invaded set once, as
`_create_dbs_on_array` already does for the stimulated population, store it as a
per-neuron 0/1 mask, and keep a separate scalar for per-pulse propagation
reliability — which also makes the 125 Hz propagation failure expressible as its
own factor instead of being conflated with coverage. **Experimentally grounded
deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** medium — the pooled BOLD sees a population mean, which is
first-order unchanged, but the network consequences and any per-neuron claim
about "which GPe cells DBS reached" depend on it.

### 4.4 Antidromic activation of the hyperdirect pathway is absent, and its pulse-locked synchrony is unrepresentable

**What BGM_22 does:** `DBS.md` "Known limitations" 2 and `TODO.md` §15 record
that `ann.projections(post=stn:putamen)` reaches the cortical drive through
`TimedArray` → `CurrentInjection`, that the rate-coded DBS rewriter raises on a
`TimedArray` (it needs a `dmp/dt` line), and that the cortical input populations
are therefore in `excluded_populations_list` — so `afferents=True` in practice
means `gpe_proto→stn` only. Additionally, `input_streams/README.md` §4.5 notes
that the corticostriatal and corticosubthalamic streams are independent draws,
and `parameters.py` sets `ci.shared_fraction_dict` to 0 for all four BG
receivers, so cortical afferents of neighbouring STN neurons are fully
independent bin by bin.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2018 is entirely about this pathway. We recorded cEPs
over ipsilateral M1 in awake rats during STN DBS at 4.5, 9, 50 and 130 Hz, and
used a 356-neuron, 50–137-compartment laminar cortical column model (Traub et
al. 2005) driven by antidromic activation of L5 axon terminals to reproduce
them. The conclusion, in the abstract: "antidromic activation of the
cortico-thalamic-cortical pathway is sufficient to generate the intermediate-
and long-latency cortical responses to STN DBS", and "orthodromic activation
through basal ganglia-thalamus-cortex pathways was not required".

**Evidence behind our choice:** in-vivo ECoG in 15 rats (10 analysed, both
Sprague-Dawley and Long-Evans), stimulation through implanted STN microelectrode
arrays, charge-balanced biphasic pulses, 90 µs/phase, 30–75 µA, placement
confirmed by postmortem histology or contact testing. R1 (1.35 ± 0.07 ms at
4.5 Hz, Table 1) was assigned to direct antidromic activation because its latency
is too short for trans-synaptic transmission and it survived anaesthesia; R2
(8.33 ± 0.73 ms) to recurrent L5 activation because it was *completely* abolished
under isoflurane/sevoflurane (Fig 10) while R1 was only reduced; R3 (27.77 ±
2.42 ms) to the L5→L6→TH→L4→L2/3 route by selective disconnection in the model
(Figs 13–14). Two limits we must state: the same paper says "Our results do not
clarify whether the cortical elements responsible for producing the cEP response
play a role in the therapeutic mechanisms of STN DBS"; and cEPs were essentially
unchanged by 6-OHDA lesion (r = 0.90, 0.91, 0.70 in three rats, Fig 9), so the
pathway is not a probe of the parkinsonian state.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** `TODO.md` §15's assessment —
"a fitted 'afferent' effect here is a pallidal one" — is correct, but the entry
overstates one half and understates the other. It overstates the loss of the
*rate* effect: BGM_22's cortical drive is deconvolved from this subject's own
cortical BOLD *under DBS-on* (`firing_rates_matlab_condition-on.npz`), so
whatever net cortical rate change antidromic activation produced in this patient
is already in the input, not missing from it — a real structural advantage of
fitting a human under stimulation, which should be stated as such. What is
irrecoverably missing is the *synchrony*: our Fig 5 shows a cortical volley
time-locked to every pulse and reproducible across a 60-s epoch, i.e. a 125 Hz
synchronous event across the corticofugal population. BGM_22's cortical streams
are independent per-bin Binomials at `r_sc = 0`, delivered at one rate value per
2.31 s TR, and the project's own `input_streams/README.md` §3 shows input
correlation is the dominant determinant of simulated BOLD amplitude
(`Var(ΣI) = N·v·(1 + (N−1)r)`, a 481-fold swing at N = 486). A DBS-induced rise
in cortical input synchrony at unchanged mean rate — precisely what our data show
— would move simulated BOLD strongly and cannot be expressed in either
condition. The tractable ask is not a spiking cortex: run the DBS-on cortical
streams once with a non-zero shared modulation and report the resulting BOLD
amplitude as a sensitivity bound, since that machinery already exists
(`make_global_p_trace`, step 2b of `model_v07.md` §7.4) and is merely switched
off. **Experimentally grounded deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** high — it is a DBS effect the model cannot express at all,
on the pathway our own data identify as dominant, and it acts on the very
quantity that sets BOLD amplitude.

### 4.5 VTA coverage is fixed while three weaker parameters are fitted, and it is nearly redundant with them

**What BGM_22 does:** `get_loss.py` passes
`population_proportion=(35 + 23) / (70 + 75)` = 0.4 to `DBSstimulator`, from
sub-01's motor-STN VTA overlap. We confirmed the arithmetic against
`experimental_data/berlin_data/vta/sub-01/`: motor-STN overlap 35 (lh) + 23 (rh)
of volumes 70 + 75. It is not fitted. The three fitted DBS parameters are
`dbs_depolarization` ∈ [0, 10], `passing_fibres_strength` ∈ [0, 1] and
`axon_spikes_per_pulse` ∈ [0, 1] (`deap_cma_opt.search_space`). `TODO.md` §16
plans a sensitivity check on `population_proportion` once a DBS-on fit exists.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2018 treats the activated fraction as *the*
representation of stimulus amplitude and sweeps it: 20%, 40%, 60%, 80% of L5
axons at 4.5 Hz (Fig 11B), reporting that the later cEP phases R2 and R3 grow and
become prominent at 60% where they are weak at 20%. Kumaravelu 2016, by
contrast, activates 100% of STN neurons and never varies it.

**Evidence behind our choice:** the 20–80% sweep is a model manipulation, and
which fraction corresponds to a given clinical amplitude is not something we
measured. But the *direction* has an independent in-patient counterpart, which
our Discussion states: "the magnitudes of later cEP phases in PD patients were
higher at high STN DBS intensities compared with low intensity (Kelley et al.
2018)" — a citation we did not read. So: the activated fraction is our amplitude
knob by convention, with an experimental consistency check on its sign.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** we would not ask BGM_22 to
adopt our sweep — a subject-specific VTA estimate is better than our convention.
The problem is structural and checkable from the code. For the orthodromic
efferent volley, the expected number of DBS-evoked spikes leaving STN per pulse
is (proportion × N_stn) × `prob_axon_spike`, and `_axon_spikes_per_pulse_to_prob`
makes `prob_axon_spike` = `axon_spikes_per_pulse` exactly at this project's
numbers, so the two enter multiplicatively. The antidromic GPe invasion uses
`antidromic_prob = mean(dbs_on)` = proportion, again times the same per-pulse
probability. The somatic term reaches proportion × N_stn neurons at strength
`dbs_depolarization`, so for the pooled, population-mean current the BOLD
monitors read, that too is proportion × `dbs_depolarization` to first order.
`population_proportion` is therefore very nearly a common scale factor over all
three fitted DBS parameters. Two consequences, the second being the one that
matters. (a) `TODO.md` §16's planned sensitivity check will return approximately
*no* change in loss, because the optimiser absorbs the rescaling — and
"insensitive" would be exactly the wrong conclusion to draw. (b) The fitted DBS
parameters are interpretable only relative to an assumed coverage, and the
project's own data show that coverage varies across subjects: the identical
motor-STN quantity is 0.40 for sub-01, (28+18)/(70+75) = 0.32 for sub-03 and
(18+17)/(76+72) = 0.24 for sub-04. We ask that §16 be reworded from "check
sensitivity" to "profile the loss along `population_proportion` with the three
DBS parameters re-optimised at each value, and report them as a
coverage-conditional statement" — the only form in which they can be quoted.
**Methodological deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** high — it determines whether the three fitted numbers can be
given physical meaning, which is the inference the project exists to make.

### 4.6 Pulse width is not a degree of freedom at dt = 0.1 ms, so the planned check on it is void

**What BGM_22 does:** `get_loss.py` passes `dbs_pulse_width_us=100` with the
inline note that the data say 60 µs and that 60 µs is below `dt`; `TODO.md` §16
lists `dbs_pulse_width_us` alongside `population_proportion` as something whose
sensitivity should be checked. In `pulse()` the comparison is
`modulo(time_ms*1000, period_us) < dbs_pulse_width_us` on a 100 µs time grid, so
any width in (0, 100] µs makes `pulse` true for exactly one timestep per period.
`_axon_spikes_per_pulse_to_prob` then computes
`clip(axon_spikes_per_pulse * 1000 * dt / dbs_pulse_width_us, 0, 1)`, so
reducing the width to 60 µs only rescales the free parameter by 100/60 while
leaving the reachable set of `prob_axon_spike` equal to [0, 1].

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2018 used charge-balanced symmetric biphasic pulses
of 90 µs/phase in the rat experiments; Kumaravelu 2016 used 0.3 ms model current
pulses. Su 2019's Discussion states that "the effects of DBS are also dependent
on the pulse amplitude, pulse width, and stimulation pattern (Kuncel and Grill,
2004; Grill, 2018)".

**Evidence behind our choice:** the 90 µs was a fixed experimental setting, not
a studied variable, and we did not read Kuncel & Grill 2004 or Grill 2018 for
this review, so we report the pulse-width dependence only as asserted in our own
Discussion with a citation trail we cannot vouch for here.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** we make no claim that BGM_22
should represent pulse width. The point is self-standing and arithmetic: with
`axon_spikes_per_pulse` free on [0, 1] and the pulse quantised to one timestep,
varying `dbs_pulse_width_us` over (0, 100] µs yields an identical model class,
so the planned sensitivity check is provably empty and would waste a run.
`TODO.md` §16 should either drop `dbs_pulse_width_us` from the check with this
one-line reason recorded, or state that representing pulse width at all requires
a smaller `dt` — which the project cannot afford. **Methodological deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** low — it saves one wasted check and one wrong sentence in the
write-up.

### 4.7 The firing-rate term is condition-independent, is added to the loss, and cannot see DBS-evoked spikes

**What BGM_22 does:** `get_firing_rate_loss` applies one `plausible_ranges` table
to both conditions (`stn` 28–80, `snr` 21–93, `gpe_proto`/`gpe_cp` 75–85,
`gpe_arky` 15–20, `thal` 15–30 Hz), and `total_loss = firing_rate_loss +
bold_loss` with both terms in [0, 1]; above `firing_rate_gate` = 0.5 the BOLD run
is skipped and charged 1.0. The probe runs with DBS active
(`assert_dbs_state(..., "before the firing-rate probe")`). Separately, the spike
monitors record ANNarchy's `spiked` container (confirmed in
`generator/Template/MonitorTemplate.py`), while DBS-evoked axon spikes go into
the separate `axonal` container — so the DBS-evoked volley is invisible to the
measured rate, and the only DBS terms the rate can see are the hyperpolarizing
shunt toward −90 mV and the axon-driven `v → c; u += d` reset. `TODO.md` §10
records the condition-independence and defers calibration to a mini-run.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2016 §4.2 and Fig 7 show model STN firing under 130 Hz
DBS fully regularised at the stimulation rate — the raster is a set of straight
lines — because §2.6 imposes one action potential per pulse. Pallidal rates are
treated differently: Fig 10 reports "no substantial increase in the mean firing
rates of model GPe or GPi neurons between low and high frequency STN DBS",
matching the firing-rate changes measured in 6-OHDA rats during STN DBS
(McConnell et al. 2012).

**Evidence behind our choice:** the STN entrainment is imposed, not measured, and
justified only indirectly through downstream responses. The pallidal
near-invariance of *mean* rate is the half with an experimental counterpart, in
McConnell et al. 2012, which we did not read. We therefore cannot tell BGM_22
what an on-condition STN band should be.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** we do not need to. Three facts
about the code suffice. (i) A rate-plausibility term with identical bands in both
conditions, *added* to the objective rather than used purely as a gate, places a
prior on the DBS-on fit that says "DBS changes no firing rate" — applied to the
very quantity the inference is supposed to read off. `TODO.md` §10 addresses the
*threshold*, a separate question; recalibrating it does not remove the additive
pull. (ii) The measured STN rate under DBS here excludes the DBS-evoked volley
entirely, so it is not the quantity either our model or an in-vivo recording
reports, and scoring it against an off-condition band compares two different
things. (iii) Where our evidence does speak — pallidal mean rate roughly
unchanged under STN DBS — it supports keeping the GPe and GPi bands
condition-independent, so the problem is narrower than it looks and concentrated
in the stimulated nucleus. The ask: in the DBS-on stage use the rate term as a
gate only and drop it from the summed objective, or exempt `stn` from the
additive term; and record in `DBS.md` limitation 5 that the probe counts `spiked`
and not `axonal`, which we found stated nowhere. **Methodological deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** high — it biases the DBS-on fit toward the null the project
is trying to test.

### 4.8 One hand-picked passing fibre absorbs the entire fibres-of-passage explanation

**What BGM_22 does:** `passing_fibres_list=[get_projection("snr__thal:putamen")]`
with the inline comment "snr__thal is actually gpi__thal, passing fibre based on
Miocinovic et al. 2006", and `passing_fibres_strength` is one of the three fitted
DBS parameters, applied both orthodromically (`p_axon_spike_trans`) and
antidromically (`antidromic_prob` on `snr`).

**What we do:** we do not model fibres of passage explicitly, but Kumaravelu 2016
§5.1 names the candidates we consider relevant near STN when accounting for
heterogeneous GPi responses to STN DBS: "the activation of inhibitory Str-GPi
(direct pathway) and GPe-GPi fibers of passage (Bosch et al. 2011)". The
pallidothalamic tract is not among them.

**Evidence behind our choice:** this is a discussion-level attribution citing
Bosch et al. 2011 (a rat study we did not read), offered as one of several
possible sources of response heterogeneity, not as an established result. The rat
entopeduncular nucleus sits inside the internal capsule and its afferent geometry
relative to STN is not the primate one, so the anatomical transfer is uncertain.
This is weaker evidence than we would like.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** we are not asking BGM_22 to
swap our candidates in — we cannot justify that from what we read. The
methodological point stands alone: `passing_fibres_strength` is a fitted
parameter whose interpretation is fixed entirely by which projection it is
attached to, and it is attached to exactly one, chosen once and never varied. If
the dominant fibre of passage in this patient's VTA is a different one — and both
alternatives named in our literature, `str_d1__snr:putamen` and
`gpe_proto__snr:putamen`, already exist in the model and are already inside the
DBS footprint's population set — the fitted value cannot be reported as "DBS
activation of the pallidothalamic tract"; it is an unlabelled residual. The cheap
remedy is to refit with each candidate in turn (one extra run each, no model
change: `passing_fibres_list` takes a list) and report either indifference, in
which case the choice is harmless and can be stated as such, or a difference, in
which case the parameter is not identifiable. **Methodological deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** medium — it affects the label on one of the three numbers
the project will publish, not whether the fit converges.

### 4.9 In BGM_22's favour: somatic and axonal DBS effects are decoupled, which our own model cannot do

**What BGM_22 does:** the somatic term
`pulse(t)*dbs_on*dbs_depolarization*neg(-90 - v)` is a shunting pull toward
−90 mV with its own fitted strength, while the axonal effect is a separate
`axon_spike` event with its own fitted probability that reaches downstream
targets through `pre_axon_spike` without the soma having crossed threshold. The
two strengths are independent parameters, so the model can express "soma
inhibited, axon firing" — and `DBS.md` is explicit that the parameter named
`dbs_depolarization` in fact hyperpolarizes.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2016 §2.6 injects an intracellular current pulse that
evokes a somatic action potential, which then propagates to targets through the
ordinary synapses. Soma and axon are one event; the somatic and axonal effects
cannot be given different signs or different strengths.

**Evidence behind our choice:** ours is a modelling simplification, and our own
Discussion (§5.4) identifies the tension it leaves unresolved — the classical
rate model predicts that increased GPi output during HF STN DBS should worsen
bradykinesia, contradicting the clinical outcome — while noting that
"experimental studies suggest that DBS activates the efferent axons of the
stimulated nucleus (Anderson et al. 2003; Hashimoto et al. 2003)" and invoking
the "informational lesion" framing of Grill et al. 2004. We did not read those
three sources and report them as our citation trail. We make no claim from our
own data that the STN soma is inhibited during DBS.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** BGM_22's decoupling is strictly
more expressive on the axis the field regards as central, and it costs nothing.
We record it as a strength with one matching caution: the *sign* of the somatic
effect is a choice already made — hyperpolarizing only, since `neg()` clamps the
term to zero below −90 mV and `dbs_depolarization` is bounded to [0, 10] — so a
fit cannot discover a net somatic excitation even if the data called for one.
That asymmetry should be stated in `DBS.md`, which explains the sign but does not
flag that it is one-sided by construction. **Difference, not deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** medium — a strength worth claiming, with one caveat worth
recording.

### 4.10 In BGM_22's favour: subject-specific partial VTA coverage, and an accidental consistency

**What BGM_22 does:** the stimulated STN population is covered at 0.4, from this
subject's imaging, via a fixed 0/1 `dbs_on_array` (`_create_dbs_on_array`, which
`DBS.md` correctly warns is `rng.choice([ceil, floor])` of `proportion * N`, not
`round`). Only the putamen loop is stimulated; GPi, GPe and STN BOLD pool both
loops with size-share weighting (`get_loss.bold_region_compartments` and the
`BoldMonitor` fallback described in `model_v07.md` §3.6).

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2016 stimulates all ten STN neurons. Kumaravelu 2018
does vary the activated proportion (20–80% of L5 axons, Fig 11B) and finds it
changes which response components appear — i.e. our own later work establishes
that partial activation is not a detail.

**Evidence behind our choice:** the 100% in the 2016 model is a simplification
we do not defend; the 2018 proportions are a model sweep, with the direction
supported by the in-patient intensity dependence our Discussion cites (Kelley et
al. 2018, unread by us). The evidence that a real VTA covers only part of the
STN is imaging, which is outside our reading list — BGM_22's own
`experimental_data/berlin_data/vta/` files are better evidence for it than
anything we can supply.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** BGM_22 is ahead of our 2016
practice and should say so. One arithmetic observation, labelled honestly as a
coincidence rather than a derivation: the DBS-affected share of the pooled STN
BOLD signal is 0.4 × 100/200 = 20%, and the same subject's VTA overlap with the
*whole* STN — all three territories, both hemispheres — is
(5+7+1+5+35+23)/(68+68+54+55+70+75) = 76/390 = 19.5%. The pooled STN readout is
therefore stimulated in about the right proportion, though it arrives there by a
different route (a 50/50 caudate-putamen split, where associative plus limbic are
245/390 = 63% of the real volume). The same files show what the two-loop split
discards: sub-01's associative-territory overlap is 12/136 = 8.8%, set to exactly
zero by excluding the caudate loop. That is a defensible and stated simplification
— the caudate loop is the free control of `TODO.md` §2 — but it should be quoted
with the 8.8% attached rather than as "DBS reaches only the motor territory".
**Difference, not deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** medium — it strengthens a claim the project can make and
sharpens the caveat on the control loop.

### 4.11 In the DBS-on stage, two of the seven loss regions are provably constant

**What BGM_22 does:** `split_param_list`'s staged on-layout frees 10 putamen
cluster scalings plus 3 DBS parameters (13, matching `TODO.md` §2), and
`apply_opt_params` applies the scalings with `loops=("putamen",)`. The objective
is `compute_bold_correlation_loss`, the unweighted mean over the seven regions'
BOLD correlations mapped to [0, 1]. Of those seven, `Cau` pools only caudate
striatal populations and `MD` only `thal:caudate`; the caudate loop shares no
projection with putamen and carries no DBS terms, and the 13 free parameters
touch nothing in it. `Put` and `VAp` are putamen-only; `GPi`, `GPe` and `STN`
pool both loops with size-share weights, so the putamen contribution to each is
one half.

**What we do:** Su 2019 defines a single scalar feedback signal — GPi
spike-time beta band (13–35 Hz) power — chosen because it is the quantity the
controller must move, and reports the plant identification accuracy honestly
(the CAR model "described only 80% of the relationship between the stimulation
frequency and the beta power"). Kumaravelu 2016 §2.7 likewise defines its outcome
measures — integrated 7–35 Hz GPi power, and the strength and duration of the
long inhibition in the GPi cortical-stimulation response — before using them.

**Evidence behind our choice:** GPi beta as a symptom proxy rests on published
correlations between pallidal spike-train beta power and motor signs (Brocker et
al. 2013; McConnell et al. 2012, both cited in Su 2019, neither read by us).
Scoring on the quantity the manipulation is expected to move is a methodological
commitment, not a finding.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** the argument needs no appeal to
us. Two of seven region correlations are, by construction, identical at every
point of the 13-dimensional DBS-on search space, so 2/7 of the objective is an
additive constant; three more are half-diluted by an unstimulated loop. CMA-ES is
rank-based and a constant offset does not move the argmin, so the fit is not
*wrong* — but the reported loss is compressed to roughly 4/7 of its nominal
range, and a good caudate fit inherited from the off stage masks a poor putamen
fit in the headline number. The remedy is bookkeeping: in the on stage, optimise
and report the mean over the putamen-sensitive regions (Put, VAp, GPi, GPe, STN)
and report Cau and MD separately as the falsifiable control `TODO.md` §2 already
says they are — which is what the free-control logic implies but the objective
does not implement. **Methodological deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** high — it is cheap to fix and it changes the number the
project will quote as its DBS-on fit quality.

### 4.12 The DBS representation has no validation target outside the data it is fitted to

**What BGM_22 does:** all three DBS parameters are free and are fitted, by
`deap_cma_opt.py`, against the same DBS-on BOLD series the model is meant to
explain. `model_v07.md` §10 records that the off and on networks were confirmed
structurally identical, and `get_loss.assert_dbs_state` guards the on/off state
at run time — both good — but neither is a test of the DBS mechanism's physiology.
No evoked-response, spectral or rate prediction is held out.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2016 §3 validates the network against Kita & Kita
2011's cortical-stimulation PSTHs in Str, STN, GPe and GPi and against measured
rates in control and 6-OHDA rats (Figs 2–6) *before* DBS is applied, and then
the DBS mechanism is judged by whether the frequency profile it produces matches
the frequency profile of symptom suppression, which it was not fitted to.
Kumaravelu 2018 states outright that model parameters were not adjusted to fit
the in-vivo cEPs, and Su 2019 selected this network for the controller study
precisely because it "replicated a wide range of electrophysiological data" it
had not been fitted to.

**Evidence behind our choice:** the validation *targets* are published rat
recordings; the discipline of not fitting them is a methodological commitment we
can point to in print, not an experimental result. Its limit, honestly:
Kumaravelu 2016 §3 concedes that several parameters *were* tuned to the Kita &
Kita 2011 evoked responses, so that study is partly fitting target and partly
validation target, and the paper does not separate the two cleanly.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** the self-standing argument is
the ordinary one about out-of-sample support — a mechanism whose every free
parameter is fitted to the single series it explains cannot be distinguished, on
that series, from any other mechanism with the same degrees of freedom, and the
project's product is a *mechanistic* statement about those parameters. BGM_22
has more held-out structure available than it uses, none of it costing a new
simulation type. The caudate loop is already an untouched control with a
falsifiable prediction (`TODO.md` §2's update), and the loss file already stores
per-region correlations and all firing rates; what is missing is a stated,
pre-registered pass/fail on them — e.g. that caudate and MD correlations must not
degrade from the off fit to the on fit, and that on-condition caudate rates must
stay within their bands. That is free, and it is the difference between "we
fitted three DBS parameters" and "the fitted DBS parameters survived a test they
could have failed". **Methodological deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** high — it is what separates a fit from an inference, and the
project's stated goal is the latter.

### 4.13 Most of the rate bands in the objective have no written provenance

**What BGM_22 does:** `get_firing_rate_loss.plausible_ranges` carries nine bands
per loop. Three (`str_d1`, `str_d2`, `str_fsi`) have a full derivation in
`experimental_data/activity_striatum/README.md`. `stn` and `snr` carry the inline
note "from [Li et al., 2015]" and nothing else. `gpe_proto` (75–85), `gpe_arky`
(15–20), `gpe_cp` (75–85) and `thal` (15–30) carry no citation at all; a grep of
the repository for those numbers returns only `get_loss.py` itself. These
eighteen endpoints both gate the expensive BOLD run and add to the objective.

**What we do:** Kumaravelu 2016 Fig 6 compares model and experimental mean rates
for Str, STN, GPe and GPi in normal and PD states against one named source, and
Table 1 and Tables 2–11 source or declare-as-tuned every other value.

**Evidence behind our choice:** the rates in our Fig 6 are Kita & Kita 2011's
rat measurements. We stress that they are *rat*: our own §5.2 states that "the
firing rates of STN, GPe, and GPi neurons in rats are much lower when compared to
non-human primates in both normal and parkinsonian conditions (Wichmann and
Soares 2006)", and that all our model BG rates are below 40 spikes/s. BGM_22's
75–85 Hz pallidal band is primate/human territory and is therefore *right* not to
follow us. We can offer no view on what the correct human bands are.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** the point is not which numbers
are right; it is that four of the nine bands are undocumented while sitting
inside the objective and inside a gate that decides whether an evaluation runs at
all. Anything that determines what a fit can find has to be traceable, and
`TODO.md` §10 is currently about the gate *threshold* rather than about the bands
themselves. We ask for one paragraph per band in the style
`activity_striatum/README.md` already sets for the striatum — or, where no source
exists, an explicit statement that the band is an assumption, which is itself
usable information for the write-up. **Methodological deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** medium — no single band will overturn the inference, but
they collectively shape which region of parameter space is reachable.

### 4.14 Axon spikes bypass the synaptic delay line

**What BGM_22 does:** `DBS.md` "How ANNarchy executes it" and `TODO.md` §14
record that because the DBS `pre_axon_spike` string differs from the synapse's
`pre_spike`, ANNarchy takes a separate-loop branch reading `pop.axonal` at the
current step, so the DBS-evoked volley arrives with zero delay while the natural
volley from the same axon arrives after 1.5–5 ms. §14 defers this, to be
revisited "only if the fitted `axon_spikes_per_pulse` turns out to carry a lot of
the explanation".

**What we do:** our pathway assignments *are* latencies. Kumaravelu 2018 Table 1
distinguishes R1 (1.35 ± 0.07 ms), R2 (8.33 ± 0.73 ms) and R3 (27.77 ± 2.42 ms)
and assigns each to a different circuit on the strength of those numbers, backed
by anaesthesia (R2 abolished, Fig 10) and by selective disconnection in the model
(Figs 13–14). In Kumaravelu 2016 the DBS-evoked STN spike propagates through the
ordinary synapses and therefore carries the ordinary Table 1 delays.

**Evidence behind our choice:** the latencies are measured in awake rats with
ECoG; the 2016 model's delays are the sourced Table 1 values. This is as
well-evidenced as anything in our reading.

**Why that would (or would not) be better here:** and yet we agree with
`TODO.md` §14 as written. Everything our latencies buy is read out at
milliseconds; BGM_22 reads out at 2.31 s, its cortical drive is constant within a
TR, and its DBS-relevant delays are 1.5–5 ms against an 8 ms inter-pulse
interval. A zero-delay volley is a real deviation from physiology, but no
mechanism in this model carries it to the BOLD signal except altered coincidence
with the natural volley, which is second order. The existing plan — leave it,
revisit if `axon_spikes_per_pulse` carries much of the fitted explanation — is
correctly scoped and ordered; we would add only that the trigger be stated as a
threshold rather than a judgement call. **Difference, not deficiency.**

**Goal relevance:** low — a millisecond-scale artefact read through a 2.31 s TR.

## What we would ask for before publication

Only demands licensed by the verdicts above.

1. **State the DBS representation's frequency scope explicitly** (4.2). Record in
   `DBS.md` and in the manuscript that the mechanism is a per-pulse perturbation
   calibrated at 125 Hz and linear in stimulation frequency by construction, with
   no threshold and no saturation, and do not extrapolate the fitted parameters
   to other settings.
2. **Make the antidromically invaded sets fixed** (4.3). Draw the invaded subset
   of `gpe_proto:putamen` (and of `snr:putamen` for the passing fibre) once, as
   `_create_dbs_on_array` already does for the stimulated STN, and keep per-pulse
   propagation reliability as a separate scalar.
3. **Bound the cortical-synchrony effect** (4.4). Since the shared-modulation
   machinery already exists and is merely set to zero, run the DBS-on condition
   once with a non-zero cortical `r_sc` and report the change in simulated BOLD
   amplitude as a sensitivity bound on the hyperdirect effect the model cannot
   represent.
4. **Reword `TODO.md` §16 from "sensitivity" to "profile"** (4.5). Profile the
   loss along `population_proportion` with the three DBS parameters re-optimised
   at each value, over at least the 0.24–0.40 range the project's own three
   subjects span, and quote the fitted DBS parameters as coverage-conditional.
5. **Drop `dbs_pulse_width_us` from that check** (4.6), recording the one-line
   reason: at `dt` = 0.1 ms any width ≤ 100 µs gives an identical model class
   once `axon_spikes_per_pulse` is free.
6. **Remove the rate term's additive pull in the DBS-on stage** (4.7). Use it as
   a gate only, or exempt `stn`; and record in `DBS.md` that the probe counts
   ANNarchy's `spiked` container and not `axonal`, so DBS-evoked activity is
   invisible to the measured rate.
7. **Refit with each passing-fibre candidate** (4.8) — `snr__thal:putamen`,
   `str_d1__snr:putamen`, `gpe_proto__snr:putamen` — and report either
   indifference or non-identifiability of `passing_fibres_strength`.
8. **Score the DBS-on stage on the putamen-sensitive regions** (4.11), reporting
   `Cau` and `MD` separately as the control they already are, rather than
   averaging two provably constant terms into the objective.
9. **Pre-register the free control as a pass/fail** (4.12): state before the on
   fit what caudate BOLD correlations and caudate rates must do for the
   inference to stand.
10. **Document the four undocumented rate bands** (4.13) — `gpe_proto`,
    `gpe_arky`, `gpe_cp`, `thal` — or mark them as assumptions in the code and in
    the manuscript.
11. **Record the two structural strengths, with their caveats** (4.9, 4.10): the
    decoupling of somatic and axonal DBS effects, noting that the somatic sign is
    one-sided by construction; and the subject-specific partial VTA coverage,
    noting the 8.8% associative-territory overlap the two-loop split sets to zero.
